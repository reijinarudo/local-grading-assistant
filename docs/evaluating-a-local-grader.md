# Evaluating a local grading model

Before trusting a local model with real grading, test it against submissions you have
already graded yourself. Pick two or three that span the range: one with few errors, one
with a common formatting or labeling error, and one with a conceptual error or a missing
file. Write down your own scores and the issues you found for each. Those are the
reference answers.

Run each submission through the model in a fresh chat, one submission per chat, and
compare the output to your reference.

## A case passes when all five hold

1. The total is within 6 points of yours, and no single rubric part is more than 4 points off.
2. Every issue you found is named somewhere in the model's output. Missing a minor item is acceptable. Missing the most important issue for that case is a fail.
3. No fabricated numbers. Every statistic the model states appears in the packet or in the brief. One invented figure fails the case.
4. Nothing you decided not to count against the student is deducted.
5. The student letter is usable with light editing: fixing sentences, without rewriting paragraphs or reordering the corrections.

## Any one of these ends the test

- The model invents a statistic, a chart feature, or a file it was not given.
- It misses an issue the packet flags in Section 2.
- It puts scores or rubric labels in the student letter after being told not to.
- It cannot finish one submission in a time you can live with.

## If it passes

The realistic role is first draft. The script and the model produce the summary and the
letter. The instructor reads the work, edits the letter, and enters the score. Decisions
about missing files or academic integrity stay with the instructor.
